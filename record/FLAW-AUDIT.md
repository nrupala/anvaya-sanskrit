## Ruler (a): the four misses are all one class — and it is not confusion

The STRICT reader misses exactly four relations, and in every case
the relation TYPE is certain from the case alone; only the HEAD needs
a convention the strict mode forbids itself:

- **g13 तस्य → पुत्राः (sambandha).** Ṣaṣṭhī says "relation" (2.3.50:
  ṣaṣṭhī is the residual case) but never says to what. The genitive's
  head is the one thing Sanskrit case genuinely does not carry.
- **g25 तान् → आलोक्य, g28 सचिवान् → आहूय (karma).** Dvitīyā says
  "karma" beyond doubt; S4 has three predicates (two absolutives +
  the finite verb), and case alone does not choose among them.
- **g26 शास्त्रविमुखान् → तान् (viśeṣaṇa).** The adjective agrees
  fully with TWO words — तान् and सचिवान् are both dvitīyā, plural,
  masculine. Agreement narrows it to the pair; it cannot pick.

Allow the three positional conventions every anvaya commentary
practises (a karma belongs to the next predicate; a genitive hangs
on the next substantive; an ambiguous adjective takes its nearest
agreeing word) and the reader is 29/29. Note what did NOT occur in
(a)'s run: no relation type was ever wrong, no edge was ever
inverted, no subject was ever destroyed. The failures are
under-determination, not error — the information is present, one
convention short of complete.

## Ruler (b): four flaw clusters

1. **Sandhi fusion — 10 of 29 relations unreachable before parsing
   begins.** The tokenizer never splits a sandhi join: सकलकलापारङ्गतो-
   ऽमरशक्तिर्नाम stays one token (3 gold words: g9, g10, g11 die),
   वहुशक्तिरुग्रशक्तिरनन्तशक्तिश्चेति stays one token (5 gold words:
   g16–g20 die), ताञ्शास्त्रविमुखान् stays one token (g25, g26 die).
   Where no sandhi joins words (S1), tokenization is perfect — the
   failure is exactly and only the joins.
2. **Register mismatch — a Vedic model on classical prose.** The
   model's root choice collapses in 3 of 4 sentences: S1's root is
   the adjective दाक्षिणात्ये (tagged VERB; the real verb अस्ति is
   demoted to its "object"), S2's root is the adverb तत्र (tagged
   VERB, imperative, 2nd person), S3's root is बभूवुः tagged NOUN
   with Case=Vocative. Feature noise throughout: नगरम् Case=Voc,
   the compound adjectives Case=Acc, राजा tagged VERB in S2 and S4.
   S2's subject edge comes out inverted (बभूव as object of राजा).
   This is the English S4 catastrophe — a destroyed subject — except
   it happens in every sentence, because the ruler was trained on a
   different millennium of the language.
3. **The absolutive chain is flattened.** S4's two ktvā predicates
   are the sentence's spine (see → summon → speak); the parser calls
   आलोक्य the object of प्रोवाच and hangs सचिवान् on प्रोवाच directly
   (g28), erasing the chain the case system had kept perfectly clear.
4. **Compound shrug.** Every compound is stamped Compound=Yes and
   left whole (defensible at sentence level — the gold does the
   same), but `compound:coord` becomes a junk drawer: in S3 the
   genitive तस्य, the numeral त्रयः, the subject पुत्राः and the
   naming predicate नामानः are ALL labelled compound:coord of
   something. The model's one nominal-sequence label does the work
   of four distinct grammatical relations, wrongly.

Borderline calls, named (the generous 4 of the 6): g8 (obl for a
qualifier — right head), g14 (compound:coord for a numeral — right
head), g24 (advcl for the kartā — right pair, subject labelled a
clause), g27 (obj for an absolutive — right pair). Recount strictly
and (b) is 2/29. The headline 6/29 already applies the English
audit's convention; no further generosity is hidden in it.

## What neither ruler was asked to do

Cross-sentence coreference (तस्य → the king; तान् → the sons) is
interpretation, as in English: it enters the graph only as 2 CLAIM
edges with their bases written. Neither ruler attempts it; the rule
reader's silence here is correct behaviour, not a miss — the gold's
29 relations are all intra-sentence by design.

## Coverage limits of this sample (carried into the verdict)

- Three kārakas never occur in the passage: tṛtīyā (karaṇa),
  caturthī (sampradāna), pañcamī (apādāna). The hypothesis is tested
  on kartā, karma, adhikaraṇa and sambandha only.
- N = 29 is small; the passage is one genre (narrative frame prose).
  The fractions are exact for this text and indicative beyond it.
- Ruler (a) was handed gold morphology. That is the task's design —
  it isolates WHERE the information lives — and the verdict states
  the conclusion in exactly those terms, no further.
